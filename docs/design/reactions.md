# Elemental Reactions & Crowd Control

## Elemental reactions (design in progress)

**Departs from Genshin's automatic pairwise model.** Towers auto-fire constantly, so a reaction that triggers whenever two elements touch the same enemy becomes ambient background noise rather than a deliberate moment. Reactions instead require a **dedicated consumer** — a specific rune built to trigger off an existing element. Incidental crossfire between two vanilla elemental towers does nothing beyond overwriting the marker.

**Three deliberately distinct concepts:**

- **Status** — single-slot, whichever element hit the enemy most recently. Purely a marker for "is there something here a consumer could trigger off." Overwritten by the next elemental hit whether or not anything consumed it.
- **Ailment** — a named, duration-based elemental effect (Burn: damage over 3s) in the Enemy's debuff list — the elemental kind of debuff ([architecture.md](architecture.md), Debuff). Multiple stack and coexist independently, since they don't need to react with each other.
- **Reaction** — what happens when an incoming hit's element meets an existing Status. Always opt-in: it only fires because a rune on the _attacking_ spell checks for it. The Status stays enemy-owned state; the decision to act on it belongs to the rune.

**Reactions are one kind of hit-time rune, not the only kind.**

- **`OnHitRune` is the general hook** — a rune that runs the moment a spell's damage lands — and `ReactionRune` is the subset that _spends_ an enemy's Status, which its `OnHit` signals by returning true. `ChillRune` is the counterexample that forced the split: it reads the incoming hit's own element rather than anything already on the enemy, so it must be socketable alongside a reaction rather than competing with it for the same slot.
- **A rune's hook receives an `OnHitContext`** carrying the target, the damage packet, the casting spell's own tags and the hit's `Attribution` (so anything the rune starts, like a Burn, is credited to the same player).
- **A second hook, `AfterHit`,** runs once the hit has fully landed — Status set, death resolved — with an `AfterHitContext` that adds the Status the hit found and whether it killed. It's for a rune that cares how a hit turned out (an execute, an on-kill effect), and it's free to deal new hits, since nothing is mid-resolution any more. Shatter is the first to use it.

**Hit-time runes must not mutate state other hit-time runes read.** Nothing in the loop writes `Status` — it's set once afterward from `DamagePacket.DominantElement` — and `OnHit` takes the damage packet by value, so a rune physically cannot alter the hit's damage (that's what `Rune.DamageMultiplier` is for, at resolve time). Together with the one-reaction-per-Ritual cap those make socket order irrelevant. Break the invariant and order becomes load-bearing, and would need a defined sort rather than inheriting socket order by accident.

**Each reaction is its own Rune**, reusing rune socketing rather than inventing a parallel customization layer — gated by `RequiredTags` and costing a socket, so picking one is a real build tradeoff rather than a settings preference. **A Ritual takes only one reaction rune**, since two would both spend the same Status on the same hit — `ReactionRune` shares one `Rune.ExclusiveGroup` across every reaction, the same mechanism that enforces one-per-type for everything else. This also lets a single pairing have multiple competing rune options later, competing for the same slot, so a new variant is just one more `.tres`.

**Ruled out:** automatic knockback/CC on incidental double-hits (too disruptive too often given automated fire), and elemental _resistance_ (same problem — a passive rule fires on every qualifying hit, not just deliberate ones).

**Wind is consumer-only** and never becomes a Status others can consume, matching Anemo. **Swirl is built into Wind itself rather than gated behind a rune** — the one deliberate exception, justified because Wind has no identity outside the reaction system at all, so a Wind spell's entire purpose already _is_ Swirl. It's a rule of the hit pipeline, not of any spell, so any hit Wind leads swirls — a Sentinel's included, which is what Overdrive's Wind shots under Attunement rely on.

A reaction rune with a disruptive component (knockback, stun) needs its own per-enemy internal cooldown (~5s) so a lingering enemy can't be re-triggered every attack cycle. Pure damage-multiplier reactions don't need one. **Overload** is why — a knockback burst firing on every attack cycle would be unbearable. Freeze and Electrocute are the same case; how hard CC is limited is its own section, below.

**A burst or one-off reaction is a marker debuff.** A rune can't deal hits from `OnHit`, since the hit is still resolving, so Melt and Overload apply a debuff (`MeltedDebuff`, `OverloadedDebuff`) that does the damage once on its first tick, a frame later, then lingers as the per-enemy cooldown — while it's there, that reaction can't trigger on that enemy. A marker has no element, so it isn't an ailment. It's simpler than hard CC's recovering window (Crowd-control limits, below) because it has no active effect to switch off. **A blocked reaction still spends the Status**, the same rule as hard CC.

**Reactions trigger on the hit's dominant element** meeting the right Status, and scale off the hit's total — Ignition's pattern, followed by every reaction since. Dominance is also what stops a sliver of an element (gear splashing 1 Lightning onto a Cold spell) from setting anything off. How a genuinely mixed hit should behave is still open (below).

**Goal:** at least one working option for every pairing below. Ignition, Overload, Frostburn, Melt, Shatter, Freeze, Electrocute and Swirl are built; everything else is WIP — named and sketched, not committed. Names are placeholders.

- **Ignition** _(built)_ — Fire and Lightning combust: detonates the latent element into a Burn DoT. Bidirectional (below).
  - **A burning enemy looks alight**, whatever set the Burn: heat crawling up its body (a `material_overlay` on its meshes, sampled in world space so the parts burn as one) and flame tongues rising up a shell just outside its body capsule to above its head. Immolation burns hotter — whiter, brighter, faster and taller. It shows on every machine, off the synced debuff types.
- **Swirl** _(built)_ — built into Wind rather than a rune (below). A hit whose largest share is Wind, landing on an enemy with a Status while another living enemy stands within 3m, takes that Status and bursts it: everything within 3m, the struck enemy included, takes 30% of the hit's Wind as that element (`GameRules.SwirlRadius`, `SwirlDamageFraction`).
  - **Each burst hit is ordinary**, so it sets the Status on everything it touches — the spread. A lone enemy doesn't swirl at all, since it would only lose its Status and get it straight back.
  - **No chains:** burst hits carry no Wind and no runes, so a swirl never sets off another; damage-over-time ticks and mirrored hits never swirl; and a Status a reaction rune already spent on the same hit isn't there to take.
  - **Runs on the host**, after the hit has fully resolved (`Swirl`, from the end of `Enemy.ResolveHit`).
  - **Visual** (`SwirlVortex`, sent to every machine): a handful of short curved strokes in the swirled element's color, scattered around the struck enemy and all turning the same way, each drawn end to end and then faded — wind whipping round in a small vortex.
- **Blister** _(WIP)_ — Fire consuming Water. A single-use mark consumed by the next non-Fire/non-Water hit for bonus damage.
- **Scalding Zone** _(WIP)_ — Water consuming Fire. A lingering ground patch that slows. It used to share Fire-on-Water with Blister as an undecided either/or; each now has its own cell.
- **Melt** _(built)_ — Fire consuming Cold. A one-off Fire hit for 12% of the enemy's max health, scaled by (80kg / its mass)^0.25 for anything heavier — about 8% on a 400kg Brute. It's limited as burst rather than lockdown: a 5s `MeltedDebuff` marker per enemy, during which Melt can't trigger there. Needs tuning.
- **Frostburn** _(built)_ — Cold consuming Fire. A `FrostburnAilment`: 35% of the hit as Cold over 4s plus a 15% slow — less damage than Ignition in exchange for the slow. Two merge their damage like Burns and keep the stronger slow. Ice Storm is its only host today, and its small hail ticks keep the damage modest; that's intended, not something to tune up. The enemy flashes navy once a second.
- **Freeze** _(built)_ — Cold and Water, bidirectional. Frozen solid for 2s — can't move, turn or attack, and a Brute's swing pauses mid-arc — then 6s recovering (Crowd-control limits, below). Pushes still move a frozen enemy. Placeholder visual: a mostly transparent pale-cyan box a little inside the enemy's body capsule.
- **Electrocute** _(built)_ — Water and Lightning, bidirectional. The struck enemy and **every** enemy within 4m carrying the same Status it had are electrocuted, spending theirs — uncapped, so a Water-soaked pack goes down together. 1s active, shorter than Freeze as the trade for spreading, then 6s recovering. No bonus damage: the spread already sets it apart from Freeze. Placeholder visual: violet lines crawling over a shell around the body, and the model jittering a couple of centimetres.
- **Being locked down is a mask of debuff types** (`Enemy.IsActionPrevented`: Frozen or Electrocuted) rather than a per-debuff flag, so other machines — which only have the synced types — agree with the host, and the visuals read the same thing. A recovering ailment drops out of the synced types, so its visual ends with the lockdown, not the cooldown.
- **Shatter** _(built)_ — Cold and Lightning. Executes an enemy the hit leaves below a health threshold (details below).
- **Overload** _(built)_ — Lightning consuming Fire, competing with Ignition for the same cell (the one-reaction-per-Ritual rule makes them a choice). A burst around the enemy: 40% of the hit as Lightning to everything within 3m, launching them into the air — straight up for the centre, tipped 1° outward for the rest (430 kg·m/s: about a 1.5m, 1.1s hop on a Grunt, barely a lift on a Brute). The burst is `Unmarked` — it sets no Status. A 5s `OverloadedDebuff` marker goes on the centre **and on everything the burst launched**, so a pack goes up once: a marked enemy can't set off its own burst, and another Overload's burst still damages it but won't launch it again — no juggling. A faint, oversized violet sphere marks the burst.
  - **Launches are general.** Any knockback with an upward part launches a walking enemy: a stiff hop that lands upright (a real tumble would need a stand-up animation per enemy type), not steering or attacking until it lands. It falls at ordinary gravity — a lowered launch gravity was tried and felt floaty. Every other push today is flat, so nothing else launches.

Freeze and Electrocute are both Water pairings, and should differ on an axis a player can plan around — Freeze trading damage for duration and Electrocute the reverse, or the spread-versus-duration trade above — or the second is strictly worse than the first.

**Another idea for an electric effect, would be amped / electrified where it increases their movement speed. Undecided, and we would need some payoff for speeding them up**

**Shatter (built)** — Cold and Lightning meeting executes an enemy the hit leaves below a health threshold (10%), and does nothing otherwise. The execute is a true-damage `Unmarked` hit for the remaining health (`Enemy.Execute`), in the triggering element's color; it mirrors through a Health Link like any hit, which is at most the threshold's worth. A role rather than a damage boost: rarely meaningful against fodder, valuable against tanky enemies, where the last tenth of a big health pool is a lot of damage. It needs no per-enemy cooldown, since an execute isn't disruptive CC.

- **Checked after the hit lands**, so the hit that takes an enemy under the threshold is the one that executes it. Reaction runes' `OnHit` runs _before_ damage is subtracted, and executing from there would break the rule that hit-time runes can't change what the others see, so Shatter spends the Status in `OnHit` and executes from `OnHitRune.AfterHit`, which runs once the hit has fully landed.
- **Bidirectional for now**, like Ignition. Lightning hitting a Cold enemy is the flavorful direction and may end up the only one; the reverse stays unless nothing better claims that cell.
- **Consumes the Status on every attempt**, execute or not — spending it is the cost of trying. Open to reversing if a wasted setup feels bad in play.
- **Bosses are immune or get a reduced threshold.** Bigger, tankier ordinary enemies may get a _higher_ one — they're the intended target.
- Wants a readable payoff — a shatter burst or ice shards — so it doesn't look like an ordinary death. Placeholder VFX until the art pass.

**`IgnitionRune` is built** and is the only bidirectional cell: it fires on Fire-hits-Lightning-Status _and_ the reverse, one rune in two cells, because the two elements combusting reads the same whichever landed second and forcing a different rune per firing order would be busywork. Directionality is per-reaction, not a system rule. **Ignition doesn't spread** — propagation is Swirl's identity, and giving Ignition its own would blur that and hand one rune both a damage reaction and an AoE conversion.

**Per-element damage is `DamagePacket`** (one float per element), not a tag beside a single number — a tag can't express _how much_ of a hit was a given element, which a Lightning-Rod-style Ailment redirecting a percentage of only the Lightning portion would need. `Enemy.Status` tracks `DamagePacket.DominantElement`, excluding Physical and Wind from candidacy.

**Chill is built.** `ChillRune` is an `OnHitRune` that applies a `ChillAilment` (30% slow, 3s) whenever a hit carries Cold damage, giving `Tag.Cold` its first mechanical identity beyond Ice Wall's tag-only one. It's deliberately a rune rather than a baseline property of Cold damage: a baseline would be free, always-on, and would **strobe**, since `Status` is single-slot and auto-firing towers overwrite it several times a second — the same ambient-noise problem that made reactions opt-in. Requiring a socket makes it a build decision and sidesteps the strobe entirely, since an Ailment carries its own duration independent of `Status`.

**Open, and decided together:**

- **A hit-time effect's magnitude should derive from _its own element's_ damage in that hit, never the total** — so Ignition's burn should scale off the Fire and Lightning portions combined (both are participants), not off `.Total`, and its trigger should loosen from "that element is dominant" to "that element is present at all," since a player who spent a socket on a reaction should get the payoff. None of that is implemented; Ignition still gates on `DominantElement` and scales off `.Total`.
- **Separately, for rate-valued effects** like Chill's slow percent — which have no natural damage scale at all — the lean is that a spell _carrying the matching tag_ gets full magnitude regardless of composition, while an off-tag spell dealing that element incidentally gets scaled by its share. That keeps a Blizzard chilling at full strength no matter what gear is bolted onto it, and confines the "adding a foreign element dilutes your own utility" trap to the off-tag case where a reduced payoff is the expected deal. Unreachable until damage conversion or a socket-compatibility override exists, so it's settled in principle only.

**Ground effects are their own family, independent of what spawns them.** A `GroundEffect` is persistent terrain at a location — it knows nothing about spells, so a cast effect, a rune firing off `IceWall.Destroyed`, or a reaction can each drop one in a line. `ChilledGround` is the first. A barrage like Ice Storm is deliberately _not_ one: a storm is a cast in progress rather than terrain, and the only thing the two share is "find the enemies inside a circle," which is a query helper rather than a base class.

**Wet Ground (sketch only, not designed).** A puddle where Lightning damage dealt to anything inside also splashes to everything else inside, at a fraction of the value. Appealing because it gives Water an identity as _terrain_ without needing the enemy-facing status effect that was rejected, and because the conduction fantasy is instantly legible.

- **Mechanically** it would be an ailment the area applies, hooking `OnHostDamaged` — the same shape as Health Link's mirror, with the splash never reaching ailments so it can't ping-pong between wet enemies. Because it _should_ set Status (below), it can't reuse `HitType.Mirrored`, which sets none — it would be a new `HitType` that sets Status but skips ailments.
- **Two deliberate differences from Health Link:** the splash _should_ set Status, since those enemies really were hit by lightning (which lets a puddle set up Ignition across a group), and it should **not** be true damage, since conduction is new damage rather than a copy of something already mitigated.
- **Open:** the payoff scales with crowd size, and chokepoints are exactly where a tower defense manufactures crowds — a cap on splash targets is the obvious lever if it gets silly.
- Needs a Water source to exist at all, so it's unreachable today.

**Shroud** is the first rune that changes how the _Ritual_ is treated rather than what its spell does — enemies neither see it nor collide with it, and it renders semi-transparent to make that obvious. It's the player-side answer to towers constraining placement: rather than removing the threat, you buy freedom from it with a socket. No required tags, so it fits any spell, and the cost is purely the slot it occupies. It needed the `Rituals`/`RitualHitbox` layer split ([rituals.md](rituals.md)) to exist at all, since a shrouded tower must stay shootable and repairable. **Open:** if it turns out to be an auto-include on every tower, the lever is breaking stealth when the tower casts, which keys its value off cooldown and makes a slow spell like Ice Storm a far better host than a fast one.

## Crowd-control limits (design in progress)

Towers fire constantly, so an unlimited hard-CC reaction permanently locks whatever it touches. The problem is broader than any one reaction: the ideas list already holds Freeze, Electrocute, Crystallize, Black Ice, Earthquake, Drown, Tornado and a Chronomancer's Stasis, and a limit that only stops Freeze re-freezing does nothing about **Freeze → Electrocute → Freeze → Earthquake** chaining one enemy into a permanent lock. So there are two layers.

**Layer 1 — per-ailment cooldown (built).** An enemy can't be put under the same hard-CC ailment again until that ailment's cooldown has passed.

- **The ailment _is_ the cooldown.** A hard-CC ailment like Frozen has two durations, authored separately — e.g. frozen for 2s, then recovering for 6s — rather than one total. After the active part it stays in its slot, **recovering** (`Debuff.IsRecovering`, [architecture.md](architecture.md)): it no longer stops the enemy and doesn't count as present — no ice drawn, not an ailment to anything that strips ailments — but it still occupies the slot. Once the recovering window runs out, it leaves the list as usual. Nothing new has to track "recently frozen."
- **A new Frozen is discarded for as long as one is in the slot — active or recovering.** Frozen overrides `ResolveAgainst` to always discard, like `BurnAilment` overrides it to merge. The default rule is strongest-wins with equal applications refreshing the duration, which for a stun is exactly the permafreeze problem: a tower re-freezing every half second would keep resetting the active timer forever.
- **Black Ice's knockdown is the deliberate exception** — no cooldown at all, its variance being the point ([spells.md](spells.md), Black Ice).
- **Keyed by ailment, not by rune.** Every source of a Frozen shares one cooldown — two different reaction runes that both freeze (Freeze sits in two cells of the reaction table, as does Electrocute), or a spell and a rune. Keying by rune would let those take turns. It also rides the existing one-per-class slot rule, so it needs no new slot machinery.
- **Per enemy, shared across every tower** — not per tower. Five towers each holding their own timer would take turns and permafreeze a lane.
- **Durations arrive with the ailment.** Like every debuff's numbers ([architecture.md](architecture.md), Debuff), the active and immunity durations are handed in by whatever applies it — a reaction rune exports them the way `IgnitionRune` exports `BurnDuration`, and can scale them by tier. A `Rune` is a shared `.tres`, so it could never hold the running timer itself anyway.
- **A blocked reaction still consumes the Status.** Spending it is an additional cost on top of the reaction's power. The rune knows it was blocked because `ApplyDebuff` already returns false when an incoming debuff loses to the one in its slot. Consequence: Status is shared by every tower hitting an enemy, so a reaction blocked by immunity still eats a Status another tower's reaction wanted — reaction towers compete over the same enemies, which pushes players to space them out.
- **No multiplayer timing problem.** Rune on-hits only ever run on the host (`Enemy.ReceiveForwardedHit`: only Rituals carry runes, and the host runs every Ritual), so every check reads the host's live state.
- **Doesn't cover non-CC reactions.** Melt and Overload are rate-limited by marker debuffs instead (Elemental reactions, above), which have no active phase to switch off.

**Layer 2 — tenacity (not built; not needed so far).** A per-enemy CC-resistance value covering every CC source — reactions, spells and Sentinel abilities alike. In play, Layer 1 alone has kept Freeze → Electrocute chains from locking enemies down too long, so this waits until a chain actually does.

- **It builds from time actually spent under CC, not from applications.** A fast tower re-freezing an already-frozen enemy adds nothing; only the seconds spent locked down count. Building it per application was rejected, since a fast-firing tower would max it out within a second.
- **It decays over time**, and **CC durations shrink as it rises**: the first freeze lands in full, a quick follow-up gets less, one after that barely registers. It shortens only the active part of a CC ailment; the recovering window after it stays as authored.
- **Base tenacity comes from the enemy's weight class** — fodder none, heavies some, bosses a lot — rather than flat CC immunities, which the enemy philosophy rules out ([enemies.md](enemies.md)) and which are dull regardless. Nothing is ever fully immune; bigger things just stay locked down for less time. It generalizes the mass-based pushback already sketched for spells like Tsunami, and could replace per-effect boss rules (Shatter's threshold, a max-health clamp) with one number.
- **Slows are unlimited.** Permanent slow is a genre staple and already bounded by slows stacking multiplicatively; only hard CC (stuns, roots, freezes, knockdowns) feeds tenacity.
- **The counter-argument, noted:** chaining CC already costs several towers and a socket each, so it's arguably a build worth allowing. Tenacity is then a tuning knob rather than a wall.
- Tenacity needs "is this enemy under any hard CC?" — a virtual `IsHardCC` on each debuff, added when tenacity is built ([architecture.md](architecture.md), Debuff). A recovering one doesn't count.
