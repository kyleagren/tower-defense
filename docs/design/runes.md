# Runes

## Triggers (design in progress)

**Triggers are authored, never assembled.** PoE's cast-on-crit and cast-when-damaged are a great archetype, but letting players pair any trigger with any payload removes too many constraints to balance.

- **Shelved: runes that carry or fire a second spell** — a Fireball releasing a Lightning Bolt from where it lands. Whether the payload takes the Ritual's other runes, how its damage is cut and how the UI shows that are all unsolved, and multiplying with Salvo and Chain is the balance risk.
- **Free-form triggers come from uniques** — "your Lightning Rituals call a strike on every enemy they hit." A unique is one designed effect, not a combinatorial space, and gear reaching into towers isn't new: Rituals already resolve against their owner's sheet. Waits on uniques existing ([items.md](items.md)).
- **Out: cast when damaged.** Rituals fire on their own anyway, so the only new thing is a reason to let a tower take hits and babysit its repairs for damage, which feels bad.
- **Maybe: a crit-triggered Ritual** that does nothing until a nearby player crits, then fires at what was crit — cast-on-crit in a form that suits towers. Not designed.
- **Maybe: cast on ability** — the Ritual fires when a Sentinel's ability takes effect within its range, aimed where it did. The trigger is the ability taking effect, not a hit, because the best case doesn't hit at all: a meteor Ritual that waits for Soldier's Converge field to pull a pack together, then drops onto the field's centre a beat later. An ability that does hit is just another place it took effect. Rewards ability use rather than crit luck, and ties a Sentinel's kit to the Rituals around them. It would share Manual activation and Resonance's "casts only when pulsed" path, the pulse being the ability's event; a ground-targeted spell aims at the event's point, a targeted one at the nearest enemy to it. Needs abilities to announce where they took effect, which nothing does yet. Not designed.

**Event runes fit:** runes reacting to something that happened — a kill, a wall breaking, an enemy dying nearby. Whether a rune hears only its own Ritual's events ("this Ritual killed a frozen enemy") or anything in range ("an enemy died nearby", "a Ritual in range broke") is decided per rune, like a reaction's direction. Candidates: shattering a frozen enemy this Ritual kills, Splinter burst, a broken wall exploding. **Ones about this Ritual's own hits fit `OnHitRune.AfterHit`**, which runs after damage lands and reports a kill — the same hook Shatter uses, and the first code that will read `Hit.Attribution` for kill credit. Ones about anything in range (an enemy dying nearby, a Ritual breaking) need events beyond a hit, which don't exist yet.

## Trigger runes (built)

A **trigger rune** stops its Ritual casting on its own: it keeps targeting as usual but casts only when **pulsed**. `TriggerRune` shares one exclusive group, so a Ritual takes one, and they fill the Trigger bucket. Incompatible with spells whose own `CastTrigger` decides when they cast (Ice Wall). A trigger that only holds a cast back until there's something worth hitting sets `AllowsTriggerRunes`, so Lightning Strike takes them — and a pulse fires it whether or not anything nearby carries Lightning.

- **Manual activation** — the pulse is any player looking at the Ritual within repair reach and pressing interact ("Press E to Activate", "Recharging" while it isn't ready; a copy asks the host). 100% more damage (120% at Legendary). Its cooldown becomes whichever is greater of double it or it plus 5s — a spell with none gets 5s, a 2s one 7s, an 8s one 16s. Pressing it with nothing in range wastes the cast: you walked over and chose to. A manual Ritual at the centre of a Resonant cluster fires the whole cluster on one press.
- **Resonance, Far Resonance and Fast Resonance** — the pulse is a cast from any other Ritual, any player's, within this Ritual's **resonance radius** (its own range, gear and runes included) **landing its first hit**. Pulsing on the hit rather than the cast means the follow-up lands after what set it off, so the source's element is already on the enemy for a reaction. Each cast pulses once however many enemies or projectiles it hits, and still pulses if its Ritual breaks or is torn down while the shot is in flight. A cast that never hits — a miss, or a spell like Ice Wall that deals no damage — pulses nothing, and neither does damage a cast leaves behind (Burn ticks, Overload bursts, Swirl, Melt).
  - Resonance: 30% more damage, +2s cooldown. Far Resonance: 25% more damage, +2s cooldown, and 20% increased range that its resonance radius leaves out — it reaches further than it listens. Fast Resonance: 8% more damage, no added cooldown. The added cooldown is what keeps Resonance from being a free damage rune beside a fast caster, which pulses it constantly; it's aimed at cooldown-heavy spells, where a cluster landing together is the payoff. Fast Resonance is the no-cost option for fast spells.
  - **It aims at the enemy that was hit** if in range and in sight, otherwise the in-range enemy nearest it. This is what makes it more than a worse tower — a Fire Ritual plus a Resonant Ignition Ritual becomes a deliberate pair hitting the same enemy.
  - **With nothing in range it still reaches toward the enemy that was hit** (where it stood when the pulse arrived, if it has since died). A projectile fires at it and flies as far as it goes, a beam or twister heads that way, and a ground spell lands on `Ritual.CastPoint` — that target pulled in to the edge of this Ritual's range and dropped onto the terrain beneath. Lightning Bolt needs an actual enemy, so its pulse is wasted.
  - **Its own cooldown still applies**, and a pulse arriving during cooldown is dropped rather than queued. A Resonant cast's own first hit pulses too, so chains cascade across a cluster — each hop 0.1s after the hit that set it off, so a chain reads as a ripple — bounded because each Ritual fires at most once per cooldown.
- **The added cooldown is one rule** (`TriggerCastData`): the greater of the cooldown × a multiplier or the cooldown + some seconds, a step after the rest of cooldown resolution and before cooldown-recovery gear, which then speeds up the added time like any other. A trigger rune's cooldown doesn't make Tempo fit, and doesn't stop Haste fitting ([rituals.md](rituals.md)).
- Host-only like all Ritual logic: each cast carries a `ResonancePulse` (where it was cast from, and whether it has pulsed yet) on its `SpellHit`; the first hit to land walks `LevelContext.Rituals` and pulses every Resonant one whose radius reaches that point. The pending pulse lives on the Ritual, never on the shared rune or the rebuilt context.
- **Seeing what a Resonant Ritual listens to** (`ResonanceOverlay`): a light lavender line to every placed Ritual whose casts would pulse it, plus — for Far Resonance, whose resonance radius is smaller than its range — that radius as an outline ring inside the filled range circle. Shown while placing it (redrawn as the ghost moves) and while holding Alt on a placed one. Lines are one pixel thick (`ImmediateMesh` lines); thicker strips are a later polish.
  - **Not shown yet:** placing an ordinary Ritual doesn't draw lines to the Resonant Rituals it would set off.

## Targeting runes (built)

A **targeting rune** replaces its Ritual's target priority and deals more damage to the enemy it picks. Targeting costs a socket rather than being a free per-Ritual toggle — the default (`ClosestToGoal`) should be good enough most of the time — and the damage bonus is what makes that socket a trade rather than a tax. `TargetingRune` shares one exclusive group, so a Ritual takes one. 1.1× mana. Incompatible with spells that cast without a target (Ice Wall).

- **Proximity** — closest to the Ritual. **Horizon** — furthest from it, still in range. **Giantslayer** — highest max health. **Cull** — lowest current health. **Swarm** — most enemies within 4m of it (`EnemyTargeting.ClusterRadius`, a placeholder), the middle of a pack.
- **Embersight, Frostsight, Tidesight, Stormsight** — enemies carrying that Status first; **Omen** — any Status. With none in range the Ritual targets as normal rather than idling. Wind never becomes a Status, so there's no Wind one.
  - **Idea, not designed:** inverse status targeting — enemies _without_ a given Status first (or with none at all), so a Ritual trying to spread Fire as widely as possible skips enemies already burning. Open: whether it's a separate rune per element, a no-Status-only rune, or a modifier on the existing ones, and how its damage bonus works when the point is the application rather than the hit.
- **Ties go to the enemy furthest along** — common for Giantslayer and Swarm, since same-type enemies share max health and pack sizes tie.
- **The damage bonus** is 10% more at Common rising to 20% at Legendary, the same for every targeting rune for now (Cull's mostly overkills and may want its own number). It lands only on the cast's chosen target — chains, forks, extra projectiles and the rest of an area deal normal damage — and applies before on-hit runes, so the number shown includes it. A status rune's bonus needs the target to carry its Status before the hit, so a fallback target gets none. Each cast carries the rune and its `ChosenTarget` on its `SpellHit`.
- **Bearing** — fires in one direction chosen at placement rather than at a target. Once the Ritual's spot is confirmed, an aim step points its lane the way the camera faces — not toward the reticle, which would swing it back at the player whenever the view lands short of the Ritual. Its range becomes a 3m-wide lane, which replaces the circle while placing and under Alt; it fires only when an enemy it can see is in the lane, always straight down it. Fits Projectile, Line and Traveling spells, so beams, projectiles and Gust. Its bonus is 10–20% more damage to everything, since there's no chosen target. The direction is `AimPlacementData`, filling the `FixedAim` component the rune adds.
- **Anchor** — Bearing's counterpart for spells that land on a spot: fits **AoE spells without Line, Traveling or Projectile** (Ice Storm, Meteor, Lightning Strike, Rain, Black Ice). Once the Ritual's spot is confirmed, the range shrinks to its outlined edge and a disc the size of the spell's area (Expand included) follows the reticle, held inside the range. It fires only when an enemy is inside that disc — no sight needed, since it isn't aiming at anyone — and always lands on the disc. The same 10–20% damage bonus as Bearing. The spot is `SpotPlacementData`, filling the `FixedSpot` component; every spot spell's cast effect lands through `Ritual.LandingPoint`.
- **Aiming reaches far.** During Anchor's aim step the reticle follows wherever the view hits the ground, out to 60m (`RitualPlacer.AimReach`), since the pick is clamped to the Ritual's range anyway — no walking over to the spot. A reticle on a wall's face steps back off it onto the ground below, and a spot clamped to the range edge is dropped onto the terrain there. Placing the Ritual itself keeps the short reach.
- **It overrides Resonance's aim.** A Resonant Ritual with a targeting rune fires at its own pick when pulsed, so a Manual Ritual can set off a cluster that each keep their own lane. With nothing in range it still reaches toward the enemy that was hit.
- Swarm's search costs in-range × all enemies per search interval; worth watching in the many-enemies test.

## Rune ideas not yet built

Every rune should hook a _tag_, not a spell class, so it applies to any future spell sharing that tag. Worth checking each idea against that explicitly. Speculative spell, rune and character drafts live in `ideas.md`, not here.

- **Prime** — a "Primed" debuff; the next `Tag.Lightning` hit on a Primed enemy sparks a random _nearby_ enemy, evaluated fresh off the struck enemy's position so no group tracking is needed. **Must be capped to once per cast** — without that it compounds with `ChainRune` worse than Fireball's Chain+Explode ceiling, which is already bounded to one shot.
- **More targeting runes** — highest share of health remaining, flying enemies (none exist yet), and furthest from the objective (`ideas.md`).
  - **The flying rune should be stronger than its siblings** — a bigger damage bonus than the shared 10–20%. High flyers will rarely be the ones closest to the objective, and by the time one is, it's already a problem, so it fills a coverage gap rather than expressing a preference.
  - **Before flyers exist, the default has to see them.** Only `WalkingEnemy` updates `DistanceToGoal`; anything else stays at `float.MaxValue` and never wins `ClosestToGoal` while a walker is in range. A `FlyingEnemy` needs its own measure — likely straight-line distance to the objective, which reads shorter than a walker's path length for the same real threat.
