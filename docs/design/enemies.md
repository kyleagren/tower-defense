# Enemies & Lanes

**Philosophy: trade-offs, not gates.** Soft pressure (tanky/slow vs. fast/fragile, grouping, timed bursts, partial resistances) over hard immunities. **No elemental resistances at all** — elemental typing is a player-facing build axis only. Difficulty tiers differentiate through composition and timing, never flat stat multipliers.

Roster sketch: Fodder, Ranged Attacker, Tank, Flyer, Sapper, Assassin-type. **Built so far:** `Grunt` (ordinary walker), **Marksman** (ranged), **Sapper** (tower-killer) and **Brute** (heavy melee).

**Enemy variety is subclassing, not composition.** `Enemy` → `WalkingEnemy`/future `FlyingEnemy`/`BossEnemy` → concrete types. An earlier plan called for swappable `IMovementBehavior`/`IAttackBehavior` objects by analogy to `SpellCastContext`'s components, but that composability only pays off when the same behavior is genuinely reused across many combinations, the way `ChainRune` applies identically to any spell. Enemy specialness doesn't work that way — a flying ranged enemy's attack would be its own bespoke kit, not a `RangedAttackBehavior` reused verbatim from a ground archer. Without real interchangeable reuse there's no combinatorial explosion to protect against, so plain subclassing plus Godot's own scene inheritance for content variants is simpler. **Still open:** whether _movement style_ (ground-lane vs. flying) is worth keeping as its own reusable axis — decide when a `FlyingEnemy` actually gets built.

`Enemy` holds only what's universal: health, `TakeDamage`, ailments, elemental-status visuals, registration, a virtual `Die()`. `WalkingEnemy` holds everything pathing, leash-chasing, wall-engaging and ragdoll-specific. A concrete subclass is only worth creating once an enemy needs code its siblings don't — `grunt.tscn` just attaches `WalkingEnemy.cs` directly.

**Movement.** Every walking enemy paths for itself: its `NavigationAgent3D` targets the objective and it steers along the agent's path, so it takes the cheapest route from wherever it stands — with travel-cost zones (Lane steering, below), that's the nearest pseudo-lane. A path query runs at spawn and on each re-path, cheap at our enemy counts. **Walking enemies steer horizontally and fall under gravity**: a baked navmesh sits a little above the real floor (0.3m on the test level), so steering toward a path point's full position would float them at navmesh height — and anything placed at an enemy's feet, like Ice Storm's chilled ground, would float with them.

**Lanes are predictions.** Each spawner computes its `Lane` once at load — the cheapest path from it to its target — for `RoutePreviewEffect`'s build-phase trail and for which way a fresh enemy faces. A fresh enemy's own first path is the same query, so the preview shows where it will walk; nothing walks the `Lane` itself.

**Spawn wave preview.** Outside Combat, a world-space label over each spawner (`EnemySpawner`) lists what the coming wave sends from it, one line per enemy type — "8 Grunt", "3 Marksman", "1 Brute" — paired with the route trail, so a player sees both where enemies come from and where they go. Per type rather than a total, since one Brute or two Sappers is what changes where to build. Lines follow `EnemyCatalog`'s order, so a type always sits in the same place. The text sits on a translucent dark panel sized to fit it. The label shrinks with distance like any world object and is hidden behind walls. Text until enemies have icons. A spawner sending nothing this wave hides its label, as every spawner does once the last wave has begun. It's summed from the wave file's `"lane"` entries, so every machine works it out on its own with nothing to sync.

**`DistanceToGoal`** (the default `ClosestToGoal` targeting) is the remaining length of the enemy's own path, from per-point remaining lengths rebuilt whenever the path changes. While an enemy is chasing, or attacking a wall or Ritual, its path leads elsewhere, so the value holds where it was.

`WalkingEnemy` runs a `MovementMode` state machine — `ApproachingObjective`, `ChasingPlayer`, `AttackingWall`, `AttackingRitual`. Leash and attack checks run on an interval, not every frame. Whenever an engagement ends, the enemy targets the objective again.

- **Leash:** an enemy within a small leash range of any player's active Sentinel — the nearest one wins — breaks off to chase and attack, heading back for the objective if the player gets far enough away. Same "break from default movement, act, resume" shape as wall engagement.
- **Re-pathing.** The agent re-paths on its own once it drifts 1.5m off its path (`PathMaxDistance`; Godot's 5m default is far enough to be pushed around a corner and then try to walk through it). **Stuck check:** an enemy heading for the objective that's trying to walk but covers under a quarter of the ground its speed should have over 2s re-paths from where it stands — usually enough to walk out of a corner it's been pinned in. Deliberate stops (attacking, mid-swing, arrived) and crowd control don't count.
- **`NavigationAgent3D` avoidance** is wired with radius pulled from the enemy's own capsule shape rather than duplicated as a separate number.
- **Known rough edge:** several enemies converging on the same destination still crowd and jostle rather than spreading out.
- **Step-up:** walking enemies use the same `StepClimber` as players ([controls_and_ui.md](controls_and_ui.md)), with `WalkingEnemy.StepHeight` (0.25m). Keep it equal to each level navmesh's Agent Max Climb, or lanes route enemies up ledges they can't climb, or around ones they could. A shoved enemy doesn't step.
- **Knockback** (`Enemy.ApplyKnockback`, an impulse in kg·m/s) moves a _living_ enemy — separate from the on-death ragdoll launch, since a walking enemy is `CharacterBody3D`-driven rather than free physics. It's divided by `Enemy.Mass` (kilograms, 80 by default — the ragdoll bones carry their own, unrelated masses), so heavy enemies move less but are never immune. **Horizontal only**: a real launch would need a stand-up animation per enemy type. A shove becomes a sliding velocity that bleeds off exponentially (`KnockbackDrag`); while it lasts, the enemy isn't steering, attacking or running engagement checks, and slides straight into walls rather than around them. Only `WalkingEnemy` implements it — the training dummy can't be moved. Repel, Converge's pull and Tank's planned Shield Slam all go through it. Gravity still pulls a shoved enemy down, so one pushed off a ledge falls.
- **After being moved, an enemy re-paths** from wherever it landed rather than following a path that may now run through a wall. The re-path waits until the enemy can walk again, so one rooted by Converge doesn't re-path every time the pull nudges it.
- **A taunt outranks everything.** `Enemy.ForcedTarget` finds a live `TauntedDebuff` (Retaliation), and `WalkingEnemy` checks it before every other engagement: while it holds, the enemy chases and attacks that Sentinel whatever else it would want, moving 1.5× faster so the taunt window isn't spent walking. Once the taunt lapses — or its taunter dies or leaves play — the ordinary checks take back over, and out of leash range the chase gives up and heads back for the objective like any other. The lookup names the taunt specifically rather than being a hook on every debuff, since nothing else forces targeting; if something ever does, that's when it becomes a shared hook.

**Spawning and group movement need a proper design pass (open).** Today spawning copies Dungeon Defenders: each spawner trickles its enemies out one at a time on `WaveController.SpawnInterval`, and every enemy then walks the same single-line lane independently. That can't express a group — say three enemies walking out abreast and holding that formation down the lane — and there's no notion of a group at all once they're moving. The same independence is behind the bunching: when something slows the front of a column (chilled ground, a wall), everyone behind walks into them, and avoidance fights over the overlap instead of the group reacting together. Worth sitting down on properly rather than patching: how waves describe groups and formations, how a group follows a lane (offset paths, a shared leader, flocking), and how it reacts to slows and obstacles. Expect it to be substantial enemy-AI work, not a spawner tweak.

**Marksman** only ever fires a real traveling `EnemyProjectile`, never hitscan. It doesn't hunt Rituals — only one it walks within 3m of, like every enemy. `EnemyProjectile` resolves damage against whatever it actually collides with rather than a target locked in at launch, so it's genuinely blockable by anything in the way. It's also the only thing that sets `DamagePacket.Origin = Projectile`; melee defaults to `Melee` for free.

**Brute** is a ~4m heavy with a club, slow and high-health, and a leash range twice the usual so it's drawn to players. Its attack is a telegraphed swing: a wind-up, then a sweep that hits **every Sentinel and every non-disabled Ritual inside a 150° arc** in front of it when the club passes straight ahead — so a Ritual it stops for, or one beside the Sentinel it's fighting, takes the swing too. A wall or the objective it was attacking is struck directly. Once the swing starts the Brute can neither turn nor walk (`WalkingEnemy.IsCommittedToAttack`) until it recovers, so stepping out of the arc during the wind-up dodges it. The swing is procedural, posing the club arm's bone from code over the walk cycle. **Known rough edge:** the navmesh is baked for normal-sized agents, so the Brute clips corners; a second navmesh baked for large agents is the fix if it matters.

**Every enemy fights a Ritual it comes within 3m of** (`WalkingEnemy.RitualDetectionRange`): it stops and attacks until the Ritual breaks, though a player in leash range pulls it away. So a Ritual in the middle of a lane gets torn down, while one beside the lane goes untouched. Only the Sapper hunts them.

**Sapper** (500 health) prioritizes towers over both the objective and the player (`RitualsOutrankPlayer = true`), falling back to the objective only once no live tower exists. **It doesn't chip at a Ritual — it channels**: standing at it for 4s (`Sapper.ChannelDuration`), a red disc growing under it, and if it's still channelling at the end the Ritual breaks outright (`Ritual.Break`). Freezing, electrocuting, shoving or taunting it cuts the channel, and it starts over from zero. A Sapper attacking the objective or a player hits normally. It skips a Ritual it can't get within reach of on foot: each Ritual measures once how close the navmesh walkable from the objective comes to it (`Ritual.WalkUpDistance`), and an enemy ignores any whose gap exceeds its `AttackRange` — so an enemy with a longer reach can still hit a Ritual it can't walk right up to.

Every enemy attack aims at `IDamageable.AimPoint` rather than a raw `GlobalPosition`, which would be a character's feet or an object's local origin.

**`TrainingDummy : Enemy`** is a dev-only unkillable target for testing Ritual behavior without fighting loot RNG or waiting for a wave. It overrides `Die()` to reset health and never registers a kill, reward or drop, and shows a live rolling DPS readout above its head. Not part of the real roster; lives in the hub ([hub_and_tools.md](hub_and_tools.md)).

## Lane steering and Earth Wall (design in progress)

**Steering lanes: travel-cost zones.** Lanes are computed, not authored (Movement, above), so steering one means changing what the cheapest path is. A level marks areas with boxes (`NavCostZone`, each with a `TravelCost`), and enemies prefer the cheap ground — a pseudo-lane that still lets them walk anywhere.

- **Godot's costs are per region, never per polygon** (`NavigationRegion3D.TravelCost` multiplies distance travelled inside it; `EnterCost` is added on entry). So zones need separate regions.
- **Bake once, split in code.** The level keeps its one `NavigationRegion3D` and one bake over `Blockout`. At load, each polygon of the baked mesh goes to the zone its center falls in, one `NavigationMesh` per cost, each on its own region; the baked region is disabled. Every piece comes from the same bake, so neighbours share exact vertices, which Godot merges into a connected map. Baking separate regions instead leaves a gap at each seam: each bake holds `agent_radius` back from where its own geometry stops.
- **Zone borders follow polygon edges**, not the box exactly. Fine for a bias; clipping polygons against the boxes is the upgrade if a border ever reads wrong in play.
- **Costs only discourage.** A long enough detour still gets cut across, so the gap between costs (say 1 against 3–5) is the tuning knob.

**Earth Wall** (`ideas.md`) blocks walking for good rather than for a duration like Ice Wall, so enemies have to route around it — maze-building. Placing one re-bakes the whole level's navmesh on a thread, re-runs the split, recomputes every lane and re-paths enemies already walking. A wall across the cheap lane needs nothing special: the detour through costly ground becomes the cheapest route. The Ice Wall objection ([spells.md](spells.md), Ice Wall) was paths flipping as walls come and go; Earth Wall only ever appears, short of the breach below, so routes settle after each placement.

- **Sealing is allowed, and breaks the seal.** A wall that cuts a spawn off from the objective still places. Enemies with no route attack a blocking Earth Wall instead; one destroyed that way can't be rebuilt until the next wave. Open: how an enemy picks which wall to break, and whether walls are otherwise indestructible.
- **Lanes become live**: recomputed per placement and per breach, and `RoutePreviewEffect` with them.
- **Re-bake cost.** Every wall re-bakes the whole level — likely fine at `level_02`'s size; worth measuring before a bigger map.
- **Host-only:** only the host simulates enemies, so only the host re-bakes.
- Navmesh re-baking on tower placement (below) is the same machinery.

## Not implemented yet

- **Wave-end safety net** — a wave should auto-end after a timeout even if an enemy is stuck on the navmesh.
- **A navmesh per enemy size.** The level's navmesh is baked for a 0.4m-radius agent (a Grunt), but a Brute's capsule is 1.0m, so its paths hug walls and corners too closely and it snags on them — most visibly while chasing a player. Godot's answer for differently sized actors is a second `NavigationRegion3D` baked at the larger radius, on its own navigation map or layer, with the Brute's `NavigationAgent3D` pointed at it. Mostly editor work, plus a second bake to keep in sync with the level's geometry.
- **Navmesh re-baking on tower placement.** Placed Rituals have solid collision, but the navmesh only bakes at level load, so enemies won't route around anything placed after. The idea is to re-bake once at the end of a build phase and once when a token deploys mid-wave — not per placement (expensive) and not never (broken pathing). Note this is distinct from the wall-rebaking dead end in [spells.md](spells.md) (Ice Wall).
